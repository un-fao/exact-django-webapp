I. Purpose and scope of the assessment

This assessment presents the project’s greenhouse gas (GHG) accounting and carbon balance, including the estimated GHG emissions and removals associated with project activities relative to a without-project (WOP) baseline scenario. The assessment supports the integration of climate considerations throughout the project cycle, from project design through implementation and monitoring, and contributes to IFAD’s broader climate mainstreaming objectives under its Strategic Framework.

The assessment has three principal objectives:

  • to inform project design and implementation decisions by identifying activities with significant GHG mitigation potential and/or potential sources of additional emissions;
  • to quantify the project’s contribution to climate change mitigation and, where relevant, assess its consistency with international climate commitments, including the Paris Agreement and the Global Methane Pledge; and
  • to support IFAD’s climate finance tracking, corporate reporting and results monitoring, consistent with the MDB Common Principles for Climate Mitigation Finance Tracking (WB, 2024) and other applicable international GHG accounting frameworks.

The assessment covers the project activities and areas expected to be affected by the proposed investments and estimates the project’s carbon balance over a {{ total_project_years }}-year accounting period. The principal indicators include:

  • the cumulative carbon balance over the accounting period, expressed in tCO2eq;
  • annualized GHG emissions or removals, expressed in tCO2eq/year; and
  • where applicable, the area under conservation, improved or sustainable land management practices.

The assessment can be presented as an annex to the Project Design Report and has been undertaken using the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) Environmental eXternalities ACcounting Tool (EX-ACT). The assessment may be updated during implementation as project-level data, implementation conditions and monitoring information become available.
