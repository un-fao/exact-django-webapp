II. Methodology

EX-ACT estimates and tracks the impact of agrifood systems interventions on greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, assessing agricultural commodities across the entire production, processing and distribution value chain. By quantifying the climate outcomes of planned, ongoing and implemented interventions, EX-ACT complements standard economic valuation and supports evidence-based decisions on climate finance and investment.

The methodology is based on the 2006 IPCC Guidelines for National Greenhouse Gas Inventories, the 2019 Refinement to the 2006 IPCC Guidelines, and the 2013 Supplement to the 2006 IPCC Guidelines for National Greenhouse Gas Inventories: Wetlands, complemented by peer-reviewed scientific literature and emission factors. Calculations are performed at Tier 1 using IPCC default parameters differentiated by climate, moisture regime and soil type, refined to Tier 2 where project-specific data is available.

The project's carbon balance is calculated as the difference in GHG emissions and removals between the with-project (WP) and without-project (WOP) scenarios over the defined accounting period, consistent with the reference-scenario principles of the IFI Technical Working Group's Framework for a Harmonised Approach to GHG Accounting (2022).

The WOP scenario represents the most plausible continuation of existing land use, production systems and management practices in the absence of the project. The WP scenario represents the changes in these systems resulting from project implementation. The target of EX-ACT is to isolate the incremental change introduced by the project, as reflected by the difference in GHG fluxes between the WOP and WP scenarios. The treatment of existing production systems and activities should be defined according to the specific project context and assessment approach, rather than being automatically attributed to one scenario or the other.

A negative carbon balance indicates a net reduction in GHG emissions relative to the WOP scenario (mitigation benefit); a positive carbon balance indicates a net increase in emissions relative to the WOP scenario.
